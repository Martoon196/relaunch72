## RUNG 1 — THE GUARANTEE (rewrite)

**Why it changed:** Your original guaranteed *speed* ("if we're late, it's free — keep everything"). Your buyer doesn't fear lateness; they fear *generic output*. And "free + keep everything" on a bad week is a financial liability on a 20/week human model. The rewrite guards the risk they actually have and caps your downside.

**Angle:** reason-why + risk-reversal. **Trigger:** loss-aversion, flipped onto us.

---

> ### The 72-hour ship. The not-generic promise.
>
> Two things we put in writing.
>
> **One — it ships in 72 hours.** Your relaunch lands within 72 hours of your accepted intake. If we're late, your revision round becomes unlimited until you're happy — on us.
>
> **Two — it doesn't read generic.** That's the whole reason you're here, so that's the one we stake the money on. If the hero assets read generic after your included revision round, a strategist rewrites them by hand. If you *still* think they read generic after that rewrite — you keep the audit and the scorecard, and we refund the rest.
>
> We don't get to miss either one quietly.

**What this fixes:**
- Guards the real objection (generic), not the vanity one (speed).
- Caps exposure: late = more revision (cheap to you), not a free nine-asset giveaway.
- Refund is *conditional and earned* — stronger proof-of-confidence than a blanket money-back, and operationally survivable.
- Keeps a speed promise, but makes the penalty proportionate.

**Audit:** Risk reversal 2 → 5. Addresses actual buyer risk ✓, believable ✓, operationally sustainable ✓.

## RUNG 2 — THE LEAD MAGNET

**The gap this fills:** Cold operators currently have no free step. First ask is $97/$997 from a founder they can't verify. The lead magnet gives them a zero-risk taste of the mechanism — and doubles as your proof-substitute while case studies don't exist yet.

**Format decision:** Not a PDF. An *interactive scorecard* — a sample of the actual intake mechanism. It demonstrates the "45 questions an agency charges a discovery day for" depth for free, and the output is personalised to *their* business (a generic PDF proves the opposite of your thesis).

### The Generic Marketing Scorecard

**Name options:**
- **"The Generic Test"** — is your marketing written by nobody, for nobody?
- **"Where's Your Marketing Leaking?"** — 7 questions, instant scorecard
- **"The 6-Year Gap Scorecard"** *(ties to hero copy)*

**Positioning line:** *Seven questions an agency would charge a discovery day for. Answer them, get an instant read on where your marketing is quietly costing you customers. Free. No call.*

**The 7 questions** (each surfaces a leak *and* demonstrates the intake depth):

1. **When a customer who's never met you lands on your homepage, can they tell in 5 seconds what you actually do and who it's for?**
   *(Positioning clarity — the shopfront test)*
2. **If I read your website and your competitor's, could I tell which one does the better work — or do you both sound the same?**
   *(Differentiation — the "loud bloke" test)*
3. **When did you last email your list? And when you did, did it sound like you or like a template?**
   *(List monetisation — dead-asset test)*
4. **Can you name, in one sentence, the exact customer who pays you best — beyond "small businesses"?**
   *(Buyer clarity — the targeting leak)*
5. **Does your marketing use your customers' actual words, or the words you'd use about yourself?**
   *(Voice / resonance — the generic tell)*
6. **If someone searched for what you do right now, judged your online presence, and rang someone else — would you ever know?**
   *(The invisible-loss leak — this is the emotional one)*
7. **Is your best work — the stuff you're proudest of — actually visible anywhere a buyer would look?**
   *(Proof gap — the work-vs-how-it-looks gap)*

**Scoring logic:** Each answer scores the leak red / amber / green. Output is a personalised scorecard: *"You're leaking customers in 4 of 7 places. The two costing you most right now are [X] and [Y]."* — then the bridge.

**The bridge (lead magnet → autopsy):**

> Your scorecard shows where you're leaking. It doesn't show *how much*, or *how to plug it*.
>
> That's the $97 Marketing Autopsy — the same seven questions taken to forensic depth on your actual numbers, graded, no punches pulled. And the $97 credits in full against a Relaunch72 Core within 14 days.
>
> **[See the full autopsy →]**

**Opt-in mechanics:** email captured *before* the scorecard result shows (result is the reward for the email). Delivers scorecard on screen + emailed copy → drops them into the subscriber nurture (Rung 6).

**Audit:** Value-engineered ✓ (does 3 jobs: captures email, proves not-generic, feeds autopsy). Not padding.

## RUNG 3 — FRONT-FUNNEL SEQUENCING

The whole cold-to-Core path, re-ordered so the free/cheap steps carry the proof load while case studies don't exist.

```

        ▼

        │        Proves "not generic" at zero risk
        ▼
[ NURTURE ]      Subscriber sequence (Rung 6) warms non-buyers
        │        toward the autopsy
        ▼
[ TRIPWIRE ]     $97 Marketing Autopsy — the decisive low-risk yes
        │        Credits in full to Core within 14 days
        ▼
[ CORE ]         $997 Relaunch72 Core  +  $147 content bump (order bump)
        │        Nine deliverables, 72hr, human sign-off
        ▼

```

**Key sequencing decisions:**

1. **Autopsy becomes the primary CTA for cold traffic**, not Core. A skeptic won't jump to $997 from an unverifiable founder — but $97 to see the quality of *your thinking on their business* is an easy yes, and it credits back. Core stops being a cold ask and becomes a warm upgrade.
2. **The $147 content bump stays as an order bump at Core checkout** — correctly placed already. "Want month two handled too while we're in here?"
3. **Pro is honestly an anchor right now.** It makes $997 look like the sensible middle. It'll become a real revenue line once Founding-beta results exist — not before.
4. **The scorecard result page and the autopsy page both carry the new guarantee** — proof-of-confidence substitutes for proof-of-results at launch.

## RUNG 4 — THANK-YOU PAGE OTO (post-Core purchase)

**The moment:** Buyer has just paid for Core. Peak momentum, lowest friction. Standard DM OTO placement — done in Relaunch72's voice so it reads as *good service*, not a second funnel.

**Constraint (the one real one):** at T+0 they've paid on faith and seen nothing. So this OTO is *low-friction and expectation-setting*, not a hard second sell. The *heavy* ascension happens at delivery (Rung 5) once they've seen the work.

**The OTO — "Lock in month two before you've even seen month one":**

> **Your relaunch is booked. One thing before you go — and you'll only see this once.**
>
> Right now we're about to go deep on your business. The single most common thing our operators come back for is *"can you keep the content coming after the 30 days?"*
>
> Since we're already in there, you can bolt on the **90-Day Content Engine** now — 60 more days of social and 12 more emails, so month two doesn't go quiet — at the founding rate:
>
> - **One-time: $147** *(this page only — it's $297 if you come back for it later)*
> - **Or $59/month × 3** *(same thing, spread — starts when your relaunch lands)*
>
> **[Yes — keep month two loud →]**    **[No thanks, just the relaunch →]**

**Why both price options:** one-time captures the decisive buyer at peak enthusiasm; pay-monthly captures the cash-flow-sensitive operator (a lot of real ones). Monthly totals *more* ($177 vs $147) — you're financing them, that costs you. One-time discount is genuinely time-boxed to this page, not evergreen.

**Audit:** On-brand ✓, one job ✓ (single bolt-on, not a stack), real price logic ✓, doesn't pattern-match to slop-merchant upselling ✓.
